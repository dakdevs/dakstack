# AI-writing patterns

Each entry names the pattern and the fix.

## Content

1. **Inflated significance.** "pivotal moment", "testament to", "evolving
   landscape", "sets the stage for", "indelible mark", "deeply rooted". State
   what happened.
2. **Name-dropping for weight.** A list of outlets or companies with no
   context. Pick one and say what it actually said or did.
3. **Hollow -ing tails.** "...highlighting", "...ensuring", "...reflecting",
   "...showcasing", "...fostering". Delete, or replace with a sourced fact.
4. **Promotional words.** "nestled", "vibrant", "breathtaking",
   "groundbreaking", "renowned", "stunning", "must-visit". Describe neutrally.
5. **Unnamed authorities.** "Experts believe", "reports suggest", "critics
   argue". Name the source or cut the claim.
6. **Stock adversity arcs.** "Despite challenges, it continues to thrive."
   Replace with the specific facts.

## Language

7. **AI vocabulary.** additionally, crucial, delve, enduring, enhance,
   foster, garner, interplay, intricate, landscape, pivotal, showcase,
   tapestry, testament, underscore, vibrant. Use the plain word.
8. **Avoiding "is".** "serves as", "stands as", "boasts", "features". Write
   "is" or "has".
9. **Negative parallelism.** "It's not just X, it's Y." Make the point
   directly.
10. **Rule of three.** Ideas forced into triplets. Use however many there are.
11. **Synonym cycling.** Calling one thing by four names in a paragraph. Pick
    one name and repeat it.
12. **False ranges.** "From X to Y" when X and Y are not ends of one scale. List
    the items.

## Style

13. **Dashes.** No em dashes. Do not swap in parentheses, en dashes, or spaced
    hyphens either. End the sentence or use a comma.
14. **Colons as connectors.** Colons belong before a list or an example, not
    as a hinge in the middle of a sentence. Rewrite so the point stands alone,
    without a comparison setup.
15. **Too much bold.** Do not bold every name or acronym.
16. **Label-restating lists.** "**Speed:** Speed improved..." repeats the label.
    Turn it into prose. A bold lead-in that ends in a period and is followed by
    new detail is fine.
17. **Title Case Headings.** Use sentence case.
18. **Decorative emoji** in headings or bullets. Remove.
19. **Curly quotes.** Use straight quotes.

## Chatbot residue

20. **Assistant phrases.** "I hope this helps!", "Let me know if...", "Of
    course!", "Certainly!", "Found the smoking gun!" Delete.
21. **Knowledge disclaimers.** "While details are limited..." Find the source
    or drop the sentence.
22. **Flattery.** "Great question!", "You're absolutely right!" Answer
    directly.

## Filler

23. **Padding.** "In order to" becomes "to"; "due to the fact that" becomes
    "because"; "it is important to note that" goes.
24. **Stacked hedges.** "could potentially possibly be argued that it might"
    becomes "may". Keep one hedge when the uncertainty is real.
25. **Empty endings.** "The future looks bright." End on a concrete plan or
    fact, or just end.

## Jargon

26. **Abstract metaphor nouns.** substrate, wedge, vector, locus, vantage,
    nexus, primitive, harness, surface, bedrock, scaffolding, modality,
    paradigm, gold-plating. Use the concrete word: "base" for substrate, "add"
    for wedge in, "way" for vector, "more than the job needs" for gold-plating.

## Plain speech

27. **Say the concrete thing.** Do not describe how something feels when you
    can say what it does. "SQL you can read" is a feeling; "`.toSQL()` returns
    the exact query sent" is a fact. Ask what the sentence lets the reader do
    or know. If you cannot restate it as an instruction, fact, or number, cut
    it.
28. **Dense sentences.** If a reader must reread to parse it, split it or drop
    clauses. One idea per sentence.
29. **Passive voice.** Name the actor: "the compiler validates queries", not
    "queries are validated". Passive is fine when the actor is unknown or
    irrelevant.
30. **Adverbs propping up verbs.** "runs quickly" becomes "is fast" or the
    number; "significantly improves" becomes the measured change.
31. **Fancy words.** utilize and leverage become use; facilitate becomes help;
    numerous becomes many; "in the event that" becomes if.
